Hi! I am Weina, a PhD student at <a href="https://www.medicalimageanalysis.com/">Dr. Hamarneh's Medical Image Analysis Lab</a>, Computing Science, Simon Fraser University. 

My research is on developing **end-user-centered interpretable AI** (artificial intelligence), and how to use it to augment doctors' **clinical decision making** based on **medical image** tasks.
<!-- It is interdisciplinary research among AI, computer vision, information visualization (InfoVis), human-computer interaction (HCI), and medicine. An explanation is a two-way communication/interaction between AI system and its users, that's where HCI and InfoVis come in. -->
I'm especially interested in using explanations for better learning, for both AI (enable AI to learn better by forcing explicit representation) and doctors (learn from those explicit representations to accumulate experience from big clinical data).

Previously, I received my **Doctor of Medicine (MD)** degree from Peking University, and underwent residency training in Neurology. I had worked in the hospital, pharmaceutical and tech company. Now in addtion to my research in training and making sense of artificial intelligence, in my life I am engaged in training and understanding natural intelligence, and [writing science fictions](https://weina.me/blog/tag/creative-writing/).

### My research contributions to human-centred AI and its explanation
In my research, I propose the **<a href="/clinical_xai_guideline.html" target="_blank">clinical explainable AI guidelines</a>** based on a systematic evaluation with existing explainable AI algorithms (<a href="/publications/#jin2022evaluating" target="_blank">AAAI22</a>, <a href="/publications/#jin2021one_map_not_fit_all" target="_blank">ICML21 workshop</a>), and a <a href="/publications/#brain_ai_clinical_study" target="_blank">user study with neurosurgeons</a>. The guidelines provide well-specified user-centered requirements and evaluation criteria to develop explainable AI for clinical tasks, and could be extended to other high-stakes domains. 

The **<a href="https://weina.me/end-user-xai/" target="_blank">EUCA framework</a>** is another tool to support the development of end-user-centered explainable AI. It provides fine-grained design suggestions for <a href="https://weina.me/end-user-xai/explanatory_form.html#feature" target="_blank">feature-</a>, <a href="https://weina.me/end-user-xai/explanatory_form.html#example" target="_blank">example-</a>, and <a href="https://weina.me/end-user-xai/explanatory_form.html#rule" target="_blank">rule-based explanations</a> grounded in end-users' perspectives in my user study. 
<a href="/publications/#doctor_user_study" target="_blank">Another work</a> built upon <a href="https://weina.me/end-user-xai/" target="_blank">EUCA</a> conducted clinical user requirement analysis for explainable AI via a user study with 25 physicians including radiologists, pathologists, dermatologists, neurologists, etc.
